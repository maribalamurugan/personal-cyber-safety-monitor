# HACKIES - Personal Cyber Threat Detection and Response

## 1. Project Overview

**Team Name:** HACKIES

**Project Title:** Personal Cyber Threat Detection and Response

**Vertical:** Cyber Security (CS)

**Project Area:** Personal Digital Safety

The project focuses on helping everyday digital users understand and
respond to common cyber threats such as data breaches, phishing links,
malicious QR codes, scam calls and social-engineering attacks.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Everyday digital users may receive suspicious messages, emails, links,
QR codes and phone calls. Users may not know whether an interaction is
safe or what action they should take.

The main problem identified is that users struggle to judge whether a
digital interaction is safe and how to respond when they are uncertain.

Possible consequences include:

- Account compromise
- Financial loss
- Personal information exposure
- Identity misuse
- Delayed reporting

---

## 3. Target Users

The primary end users are:

- College students
- Young digital users
- People who regularly use email
- Messaging application users
- QR payment users
- Social media users

These users may have limited cybersecurity awareness and may be unsure
about how to verify suspicious digital interactions.

---

## 4. Common Threat Situations

The project focuses on situations such as:

- Suspicious emails
- Phishing links
- Unknown QR codes
- Scam or suspicious phone calls
- Social-engineering attempts
- Possible data-breach notifications

---

## 5. Current User Workflow

When a user receives a suspicious message, email, link, QR code or
phone call, they may:

1. Check the sender or visible information.
2. Look at the wording or URL.
3. Search online for information.
4. Ask friends or other people for help.
5. Contact support if available.
6. Ignore the content.

The remaining problem is that users may still be uncertain about whether
the interaction is genuine and may not know what action to take or where
to report it.

---

## 6. Current Alternatives

Users may currently depend on:

- Security warnings
- Spam filters
- Bank alerts
- Cybersecurity awareness programs
- IT support
- Official reporting mechanisms
- Friends or peers

These alternatives provide some protection, but users may still lack
clear verification and reporting guidance.

---

## 7. Project Direction

The project is exploring a personal cyber-safety platform that could
eventually provide support for:

- Data-breach awareness
- Suspicious link detection
- QR-code safety checking
- Suspicious-call awareness
- Cyber-threat explanation
- Incident-response guidance
- Guidance toward appropriate cybercrime reporting channels

These are potential Phase 2 directions and are not presented as
validated final solutions.

---

## 8. Phase 1 Focus

The current Phase 1 focus is customer and problem validation.

The team is trying to understand:

- Who experiences the problem?
- What happens when the problem occurs?
- What do users currently do?
- What difficulties do users face?
- What consequences can occur?
- Whether the problem is significant enough to address.

---

## 9. Privacy and Safety

The project follows responsible cybersecurity practices.

The project will:

- Collect only necessary information.
- Avoid collecting passwords and OTPs.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
- Use anonymized evidence for public assessment.
- Obtain permission before collecting user-related evidence.
- Avoid unauthorized security testing.
- Respect user privacy and data protection.

---

## 10. Phase 2 Concept Directions

The current concepts being considered are:

### A. Personal Cyber Safety Assistant

A unified platform that could monitor known data breaches, analyze
suspicious links and QR codes, and guide users after an incident.

### B. AI Cyber Incident Assistant

An assistant that could help users understand suspicious messages or
calls and guide them toward safe response and reporting.

### C. Cyber Threat Early-Warning System

A monitoring approach that could notify users about newly reported
breaches and suspicious interactions.

The final concept will be selected based on user evidence, desirability,
feasibility, responsibility and value.
